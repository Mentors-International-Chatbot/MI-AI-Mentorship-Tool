## Finding: this is a four-way disagreement, not the two-way one the L0 report described

The Auth & Login plan's L5.1 cites the August incident as one disagreement (`Socio.mentorId` vs a
`ParticipantProfile` join). The real count today is **four independent implementations of "this
mentor's socios,"** each with different edge-case behavior, plus three more inline duplicates of a
narrower one-off check:

| # | Implementation | Where | Unassigned-socio behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `getSociosForMentor(organizationId, mentorId)` | `tenantPrismaRepo.ts:1711` | **Excludes** — strict `mentorId` match only |
| 2 | `mentorCaseloadScope(mentorId)` | `lib/auth/mentorCaseloadScope.ts` | **Excludes** — same strict match, plus its own ambiguous-org detection |
| 3 | `verifyMentorOwnership(socioId)` | `lib/auth/ownership.ts` | **Includes**, org-wide — any mentor in the socio's org may act on an unassigned socio |
| 4 | `getUnresolvedFlagsByMentor(mentorId)` | `prismaRepo.ts:550` | **Excludes** — strict match, same as #1/#2 |

\#1 backs `/dashboard/learners` and `/dashboard/alerts`' caseload zones — what a mentor sees as their
roster. \#3 backs every `/api/mentor/socios/[id]/*` and `/api/dashboard/socios/[id]/*` route (message,
overrides, summaries, the D.3 assistant and its confirm route) — what a mentor may act on. **These
disagree on unassigned socios by design, not by accident** — #3's fallback exists so a Canvas-provisioned
or `/join`-signup learner isn't permanently unreachable — but the practical effect is the same shape as
the August bug: a mentor can be *authorized* to message, adjust overrides, or run the assistant against
an unassigned learner who never appears in their own roster or alerts zones. Not hypothetical — it's
the documented, intentional behavior of #3 today.

\#2 is a fifth wrinkle: built for D.1's `resolveAdminScope` (course-admin/mentor scope resolution), it
re-derives organization from the caseload's own `ParticipantProfile` anchors rather than
`MentorProfile.organizationId` — a *third* org-resolution rule alongside `getOrganizationIdByMentorId`
(used by #1's callers) and `mentorAnchor.ts`'s `resolveMentorOrg` (below).

\#4 is currently **dead code** — `/api/dashboard/flags` has no UI caller (grepped, zero hits) — but it
is live, reachable, correctly-wired code, not a MentoringRelationship-shaped false lead. If it stays
unmigrated when L5 lands, it becomes a fifth definition the day someone wires a client to it.

## Classification

**Visibility** (who this mentor may see or act on → moves to the `Enrollment ⋈ CourseStaffAssignment`
join):

1. `getSociosForMentor` — mentor's own roster (`/dashboard/learners`, `/dashboard/alerts` zones)
2. `mentorCaseloadScope` — `resolveAdminScope`'s mentor-scope branch (D.1)
3. `verifyMentorOwnership` — every per-socio mentor API route's authorization gate
4. `getUnresolvedFlagsByMentor` — `/api/dashboard/flags` (dead caller, live code)
5. Three flag-action inline checks (acknowledge/resolve/snooze routes)
6. `dashboard/learners/[id]/page.tsx`'s page-level gate
7. `admin/socios/filters.ts`'s `where.mentorId` — **not mentor-facing**: an admin/course_lead filtering
   the global socios list by a specific mentor for review. Orthogonal to L10b; can stay as-is or move to
   filtering by `CourseStaffAssignment` membership later, at Phase D's discretion, not L5's.
8. `admin/learners/page.tsx`'s assignment dropdown (`value={s.mentorId}`) — same category as #7, reads
   the current value to populate an admin-only select; not a mentor's own visibility.

**Routing** (whose inbox/batch something lands in → may keep a caseload pointer, per L5.3's "ship
shared, filter later" recommendation):

9. `messaging/handler.ts:796` — escalation log line naming the assigned mentor. **Inert** — a
   `console.log`, no actual notification dispatch exists downstream. Lowest-stakes item here; moving or
   not moving it changes nothing observable today.
10. `cron/summaries/route.ts` — **re-examined, and it's narrower than "routing" implied.** Re-reading it:
    it does not batch or deliver by mentor at all — every ACTIVE socio gets its own `Summary` row,
    unconditionally, in the same loop. `mentorId` is used for exactly one thing: looking up that
    mentor's `preferred_language` as a proxy for what language to write the summary's phrasing in
    (falls back to `'es'` if unassigned). No inbox, no caseload filter, nothing a "shared vs split"
    decision actually applies to.

**Check against the shared-model decision (L5.3 recommends shared):** the question was whether a long
routing list would mean the shared decision hadn't propagated into this classification. It's a short
list, and neither entry survives scrutiny as a real caseload filter — #9 is inert, #10 is a
language-lookup proxy, not a delivery grouping. **There is no reader left that filters a roster or inbox
by caseload for delivery purposes** — consistent with "shared" already being the right call, not a sign
the classification under-applied it. Separate, smaller observation while re-reading #10: keying a
socio's own summary language off their *mentor's* preference, when it exists only as an
availability-of-signal artifact, is worth revisiting in favor of `Socio.language` directly (which
already exists) regardless of what L5 does — not part of this classification's scope, noted so it
isn't lost.

**A third category L0.2.6's own two buckets don't cover — org-anchoring:**

11. `mentorAnchor.ts`'s `resolveMentorOrg`/`getMentorAnchorInputs` — the `assigned_socios` signal infers
    a mentor's organization from the orgs of socios currently pointing `mentorId` at them. Neither
    visibility nor routing — it's how a mentor's own tenant gets resolved at all. L10/L0.2.7 already
    name the fix: anchor from the mentor's first assigned **course** (`CourseStaffAssignment`) instead,
    which is available earlier (at course-assignment time, before any specific learner exists) and more
    truthful (a course assignment is a deliberate act; an inferred socio-org signal is not). This
    migrates naturally once `CourseStaffAssignment` exists — no separate design needed, but it must be
    included in the same reader-migration commit per L5.1's own rule, not treated as out of scope.

**The write path, not a reader but what L5 actually replaces:**

12. `PATCH /api/admin/socios` (`admin/socios/route.ts`) — sets `Socio.mentorId` directly, admin-only
    today. L11 already names this route as needing `requireCourseAdmin` once `CourseStaffAssignment`
    exists. Confirmed here: no other write path sets this column (grepped `mentorId:` across write
    contexts — the only other `mentorId:` assignments are on `MentorProfile`/`LtiIdentity`, different
    columns).

## What this means for pulling L5's assignment half forward

All eight visibility-classified readers (1-8) collapse to one shared query — but per Michael's
2026-09-06 direction, the collapse and the semantic swap are **two separate stages, not one commit**,
using the A.4 → A.5 → A.6 shape Phase A already proved:

1. **Table + backfill.** `CourseStaffAssignment` schema, migration, and a backfill deriving rows from
   today's `Socio.mentorId` + course data. Nothing reads the table yet.
2. **Collapse.** All eight readers (plus the three inline duplicates) retarget to one shared function —
   but that function still implements the *old* semantics (strict `Socio.mentorId` match). Behavior-
   preserving; the existing test suite is the proof. Four implementations → one, with nothing about what
   any caller sees changing. Real, permanent value on its own even if the table were abandoned tomorrow.
3. **Dry-run diff (the gate).** Compute old-semantics vs. new-join-semantics visibility for every mentor
   without switching anything live. Three deltas expected — see below; only one is a plausible stop-ship.
4. **Swap.** The one shared function's implementation flips from old semantics to the join. One-function
   change, readable diff, isolated from stage 2's refactor — a wrong roster after this stage tells you
   which half broke it, which collapsing and swapping together would not.
5. Retarget remaining call sites, `requireCourseAdmin` on `PATCH /api/admin/socios` per L11, then UI.

The org-anchoring reader (11) moves in stage 4 alongside the others, not as a follow-up — L10 already
decided the direction (anchor from course, not socio).

### The dry-run diff's three expected deltas (stage 3)

- **Widens** (intended, L5.3's shared model): a learner assigned to another mentor in the same course
  becomes visible. Must be sized with real numbers before flipping — a mentor going from a caseload of
  12 to 60 should be a decision made on purpose, not discovered after the swap.
- **Narrows** (intended, L5.4 arriving early): the `mentorId === null` org-wide fallback
  (`verifyMentorOwnership`) goes away. An unassigned socio in a course this mentor doesn't hold becomes
  unreachable by *any* mentor, not just this one — the fallback L5.4 already planned to delete, just
  landing at migration time instead of UI time.
- **Breaks** (expected zero — the only one that's a stop-ship if it isn't): any socio with no ACTIVE
  `Enrollment` vanishes from every mentor's view under the join. This report leaned on Phase A's A.3
  backfill (covered chat-surface socios too, per `scripts/backfill-phase-a3.ts`) as evidence this is
  safe. Michael's correction: "the Phase A backfill covered chat-surface socios in August" and "every
  socio has an active enrollment right now" are different claims — three months of LTI provisioning and
  `/join` signups sit between them. **Re-run the zero-orphans assertion against production before stage
  3, not dev, and not by inference from Phase A's history.**
