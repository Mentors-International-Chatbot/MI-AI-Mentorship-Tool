SO, WHAT EXACTLY IS A BUDGET?
It's a plan you write down to figure out
how much money is coming in, how

WHAT DOES SAVINGS MEAN?
Set aside a specific percentage of
your earnings before you start
spending, rather than just saving
whatever is left at the end. (Aim for
about 10% of your income.)
TYPES OF COSTS:
Essentials: groceries, housing, bills, travel, healthcare.
From the business: supplies, materials, transport, permits.
Unnecessary: eating out, ornaments, fun activities.
STEP BY STEP: CREATING YOUR BUDGET
Jot down all the money you made this month.
Create a list of your monthly expenses, both fixed and variable.
Sort your expenses into two categories: essential and non-essential.
Figure out how much you can set aside (ideally, aim for at least 10% of your earnings).
Make sure your income is sufficient. If it isn't, consider cutting back on your expenses.WHAT EXACTLY IS AN EMERGENCY FUND?
It's a unique savings plan designed for those
unexpected expenses like health issues, home
repairs, or accidents. It helps you avoid falling into
debt. (The goal is to save up three months' worth of
expenses.)
Sometimes we earn a decent income, but by the
middle of the month, we’re left wondering where all
the money went. Without any savings, even a small
emergency can turn into a big issue. And if we don’t
stick to a budget, it’s all too easy to overspend and
end up short.
This tool is great for planning and reviewing your week. It helps you see where your money

BREAK-EVEN POINT
What exactly is the break-even point?
It's the number of products or services you need to sell to cover all
your costs. After that, you begin to make a profit.
The break-even point lets you figure out the minimum sales you need
to keep your business running.
You can sell a ton but still end up in the red if you’re not aware of how
much you need to sell to cover all your costs. The break-even point is
basically the least you need to sell to steer clear of a loss.
If you sell less, your business takes a hit. If you sell more, you come
out on top.
To figure it out, you need to start by understanding the two kinds of
expenses.
Types of costs:
Variable/Direct Expenses: This is
what you spend on buying or making
each product, like materials and
ingredients.Fixed / Indirect Expenses: These
are the costs you incur regardless of
whether you make any sales, like
rent, internet, and utilities.
Secondly, it's important to understand your product margin. You can figure this out by taking your
product revenue and subtracting the direct expenses.
Margin equals the product's selling price minus the direct expense for each unit.
Once you have the margin value for each product, you can determine the break-even point with this
formula. 
Break-even point = Total fixed costs / Margin
Understanding your balance point can assist you in:
Find out if your business is thriving or struggling.
Establish achievable sales targets.
Be mindful when setting prices.
Make smarter choices to expand.

The Weighted Average Margin is basically the average you get when you multiply the margin per
unit by the estimated sales share. So, the formula for the Break-even Point is: Fixed Expenses
divided by the Weighted Average Margin.
Case study:
Fixed costs: $500         Selling price for each garment: $25          Cost to purchase each garment: $15
500 / (25 − 15) = 500 / 10 = 50 garments
